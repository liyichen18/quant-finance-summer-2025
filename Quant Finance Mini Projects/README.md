This project aggregates four quantitative finance mini-projects focused on portfolio construction, return distribution analysis, option sensitivity, and hedging performance under realistic market conditions. First, I constructed high- and low-volatility stock portfolios and observed that higher volatility yielded greater returns but with significantly larger risks. Second, I tested the normality assumption of asset returns, finding that portfolios can approximate normal distributions, but individual stocks exhibit heavier tails, implying underestimated tail risks. Third, I analyzed Black–Scholes option Greeks, illustrating Delta, Gamma, and Vega sensitivities, especially around at-the-money strikes. Finally, I simulated delta hedging under stochastic volatility models (Regime-Switching, Heston, GARCH), revealing that delta hedging alone cannot eliminate volatility-induced losses due to model misspecification and tail events. Overall, this project highlights the importance of understanding underlying assumptions, distributional properties, and residual risks when managing portfolios or trading options.
